Matteo Stockinger

I am a PhD researcher in relativistic astrophysics at the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (Albert Einstein Institute) in Potsdam, working with Masaru Shibata.

My background combines physics at École Normale Supérieure (ENS Ulm) and fundamental mathematics at Sorbonne Université. I expect to complete my PhD in summer 2027.

What drives my research

I am interested in how machine learning can accelerate scientific research while preserving physical understanding. Many physical questions require expensive calculations across a wide range of scales or parameters. I would like to develop reduced descriptions that make these questions more accessible and whose assumptions can be examined and tested.

My particular interests include symbolic and neuro-symbolic methods for identifying interpretable physical relations, physics-informed learning, and neural operators used alongside conventional numerical solvers. A central question for me is which information a reduced model needs to retain—and when additional variables, spatial information, or memory become essential.

Relativistic astrophysics provides a concrete starting point for this work, but my broader interest is in AI as a tool for scientific discovery across different fields.

Current and previous work

Tidal resonances and compact objects

With Masaru Shibata, I have studied tidal resonances of stars on inclined, precessing orbits around Kerr black holes, moving from incompressible stellar models to compressible affine descriptions.

Black-hole perturbation theory

I am investigating gravitational radiation and quasinormal-mode excitation in parabolic encounters with Kerr black holes, including how to distinguish the black hole’s damped response from continued orbital driving.

Physics-informed machine learning

I am developing physics-informed neural-network methods for compact-object magnetospheres in the force-free approximation. This project has given me practical experience with ML tools and motivated my broader interest in their scientific applications.
